Lani Orosco, co‑founder22 February 2025

February 22, 2025

We’ve gotten the blog back up, and have a new and up‑to‑date Missions for Orphans website. In writing the blog, I hope to explain and describe all the things we try to do at the Kipsaina Boy’s Home and the surrounding village.

Lots of things have changed since we first came to Kitale in 2012. We still have 7 acres of land, but now we have 8000 tea plants on part of it (Kenyan tea is the best), with the remainder a large assortment of vegetables and grains for the boys and also for widows that stop by in need. We have a church building, a home for the younger boys, dormitory for the older ones, sleeping quarters for visiting pastors and plenty of storage buildings.

In the last 13 years, the boys have definitely grown up. In 2012, we had many that were already out of high school, a few in high school, and more in elementary school. Most of the older boys had withdrawn from high school and were in need of jobs. Those still in high school had very poor grades, which is mostly from the lack of a good curriculum. We have learned from that. Early, basic and good schooling is needed for our boys to succeed. Starting at the elementary level.

By the time our boys were in high school or already graduated, there was little to help them get ahead. We offered them work on the farm, small businesses or even a piki (motorcycle) to use as a taxi.

Sadly, these were not the answer and it has taken time to figure out what is.

It starts with sending them to better schools at the very beginning.

Schooling is expensive here. There is no free school. Elementary school requires payments along with the required food for the student to eat. Regular high school is approximately $100 a year, per student. But the problem with the village schools is the lack of funds, large classes with no books, no resources and a poor curriculum. Any further education is even more expensive.

But this blog is about the positive results we have finally achieved. And the young men who have accomplished more than they thought possible.

I want to introduce our successes. Those that have overcome the obstacles in their lives, and now have a hope and a future.

For Missions for Orphans, this is a huge victory. We now have our younger boys in progressive schools that provide a much better environment, an assortment of courses, established habits and goals. Hopefully, we will eventually have high school graduates with grades and accomplishments that provide scholarships. It has been a learning experience, but watching our boys find out about and achieve goals in life is such a blessing.

From Kitale,

Mama Lani
